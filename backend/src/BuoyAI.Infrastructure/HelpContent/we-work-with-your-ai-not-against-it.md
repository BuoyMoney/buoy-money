Something is happening in personal finance that's easy to miss under the noise. The largest
AI companies are quietly building money features straight into their own assistants:
connect your bank to a chatbot, see your net worth in the same window where you draft
emails. The pitch is convenience. The strategy is gravity. Pull your financial life into
one company's app and keep it there.

We're making the opposite bet, and we think it's the right one for you.

## Your money shouldn't be a reason to switch apps

The "everything app" vision asks you to move, to do your budgeting where you already do your
chatting, because that company would like to own both. But you didn't choose your AI
assistant to manage your money, and you didn't choose your budgeting app to answer every
question you have. Forcing them into one product usually means one of the two gets worse.

Buoy takes a smaller, more respectful position: use the AI you already trust, and let it
reach your money on your terms. With
[Buoy Money Connect](/blog/ask-your-ai-about-your-budget), you link your Buoy account to
ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini and just ask how your budget is going. The assistant reads your
real numbers and answers, and because Buoy teaches it how count-up budgeting actually works,
the advice sounds like Buoy, not like a generic finance bot.

No migration. No new app to learn. No handing your entire financial life to whichever
assistant happens to be winning this quarter.

## Open beats owned

There's a quiet principle underneath this. When a company builds finance *into* its
assistant, your data lives inside their walls, on their terms, subject to their roadmap.
When an app like Buoy *connects* to your assistant through an open standard, you stay in
control of the pieces.

The connection is read-only, so the assistant can look but never touch. It can't move money
or change a thing. You approve each connection by name, and you can cut it off in one tap.
Your data stays yours, in Buoy, and only reaches an assistant when you point it there.

That's not just a privacy nicety. It's a bet that the future of AI and money is **plural**:
many assistants, many tools, connected by open standards, rather than a single company
owning your accounts and your chat window at once.

## Build the good app. Meet people where they are.

We'd rather be excellent at one thing, helping you budget in a way that actually feels good,
and then show up wherever you already think about your money, than try to become the one app
you're not allowed to leave.

So no, Buoy isn't trying to become your AI assistant. It's trying to be the budget your AI
assistant is smart enough to ask about. Connect it once, keep the tools you love, and let
them work together, on your terms, not anyone's land grab.
