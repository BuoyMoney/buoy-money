If you are searching for a YNAB alternative, you have probably already met the two
things people say about it: it works, and it is a lot.

Both are true. YNAB has changed how a great many people handle money. It also has one of
the steepest learning curves in personal finance software, and a fair number of people
who quit it did not quit because it failed. They quit because keeping up with it became
another job.

Before you switch to anything, it is worth understanding what you are actually switching
away from.

## Zero-based budgeting, in one sentence

YNAB is built on giving every dollar a job. Money arrives, you assign all of it to
categories, and your budget is balanced when nothing is left unassigned. When you
overspend somewhere, you cover it by moving money from somewhere else.

That model is genuinely powerful. It forces you to make decisions in advance instead of
discovering them in a statement three weeks later.

It is also demanding. Every unassigned dollar is an open task, every overspend is a
reconciliation, and the system only tells you the truth if you keep feeding it. That
maintenance is exactly where most people fall off.

## Count-up budgeting, in one sentence

Buoy runs the other way around. Each spending category earns a small allowance every
day, and the number goes **up** when you underspend.

Skip the coffee and you watch a category grow. There is no pile of unassigned money
waiting to be sorted, and no month-end reckoning where a red number tells you how you
did. The feedback is daily and the direction of travel is positive.

The practical difference is what happens when you ignore the app for a week. A zero-based
budget accumulates a backlog you have to work through. A count-up budget just keeps
accruing, and you pick it up where you left off.

## What each one is actually good at

**YNAB is better if** you want maximum control, you enjoy the ritual of assigning money,
and you are budgeting your way out of a specific hole where every dollar genuinely needs
a name. The method is rigorous and the community around it is excellent.

**Buoy is better if** you have tried strict budgeting and stopped, or you want something
that survives a busy fortnight without punishing you for it. The design goal is a system
you still use in month six.
