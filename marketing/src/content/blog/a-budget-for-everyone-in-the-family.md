Shared money is where most budgeting apps quietly fall apart. You can have *one* family
budget that nobody feels ownership of, or *separate* budgets that don't add up to anything.
Neither teaches a teenager how money works, and neither gives a partner breathing room
without a spreadsheet and a hard conversation.

Buoy takes a third path. Each family member gets a personal budget that grows out of the
family budget itself. One line in the family budget becomes that person's whole world:
their allowance, their categories, their cards. And the family still sees how it all fits
together.

## An allowance that counts up

Say you give your 16-year-old a monthly allowance. In Buoy, that's a single category in the
family budget. Call it *"Riley's Budget."* The moment you turn it into a personal budget,
Riley gets their own space. Their allowance lands there, it earns a daily allowance that
[counts up](/blog/why-your-budget-should-count-up) just like every Buoy category, and Riley
can slice it into their own subcategories (coffee, games, gas) however they think about
their money.

Here's the part parents love: you set the allocation, and Riley runs the budget. You decide
the number. Riley decides what a coffee habit is worth versus a new game, moves their own
transactions between categories, and watches their allowance build when they hold back. It's
a real budget with real stakes, inside guardrails you control.

## Their own income, too

An allowance is a starting point, not a ceiling. When Riley gets a summer job, that paycheck
can flow into the *same* personal budget from their own account, on top of the family
allocation. Now they're managing allowance and earnings in one place, learning exactly the
skill you want them to have before they leave home.

The two numbers stay honest. The family budget tracks what the family put in. Riley's own
income is theirs, added on top. Nobody's confused about who contributed what.

## The family still sees the whole picture

Because a personal budget is a *child* of the family budget, the top-level view never loses
the plot. Every allocation rolls up. You can see, at a glance, how much of the family's
money is committed to each person, without opening five separate apps or asking anyone to
send you a screenshot. The shared budget stays shared, the personal budgets stay personal,
and they're the same system, not a workaround.

## Why this beats "just give them a card"

A prepaid card teaches spending. It doesn't teach *budgeting*, the part where you decide in
advance what money is for and feel the small win when you don't spend it. Buoy's family
allocations put a real, count-up budget behind the card. The card is where the money goes
out. The budget is where the thinking happens.

It works for couples, too. A shared household budget with a personal allocation each, plus
whatever you each earn on the side, means autonomy without secrecy, and no monthly argument
about whose turn it was to update the spreadsheet.

## Getting started

If you're on a family plan, open any family budget category and choose **Make this a
personal budget**. Or create a new personal budget and pick which family category funds it,
and Buoy will make one for you if it doesn't exist yet. Set the allowance, hand it off, and
let each person build their own count-up habit inside the budget you all share.

Money is one of the most important things we teach the people we love. Buoy just makes the
lesson feel less like a lecture and more like a game you can win together.
